Where each association stereotype may be anchored

A grid per stereotype: a row for each nature admissible at the first position, a column for each nature at the second. Every grid is one ontological category against another — substantials, aspects, endurants, perdurants, concrete individuals — filled out pair by pair because a lookup wants pairs, not because anything in it is arbitrary. @constitution alone has a different shape: it closes over the categories rather than crossing two, since what constitutes something belongs to its own. A test asserts both, so a pair added by hand that completes no category fails the build. A tick is what the table admits; a filled dot (●) is narrower and says something about the editor rather than about the ontology — it marks the pair for which this stereotype is offered first when a relation carries none. Several usually fit: event:event parthood suits @participational, @structural and @temporal equally, and all three are offered, recommended one first. Every stereotype is writable at any ✓ pair, dot or no dot. For parthood the table reads whole → part, and which end is which comes from the aggregation diamond, not the writing order — so a <>-- puts the whole at the source. Parthood written without a stereotype

A diamond states parthood whether or not a stereotype follows it, and tonto names none for the parthood of a relator, a quality or a mode — so an unstereotyped diamond answers to this grid rather than to any row above. Outside the substantials parthood does not cross natures: a part of an event is an event, of a situation a situation, of a quality a quality. Two things widen that. A collective admits members of any substantial nature. And a process, being a variable embodiment that endures while what it gains over time are events, has events as parts — the one pair that crosses categories by construction. Derived from the grids above, so a row added there widens this with it.

What cannot change

An association end is a property of the classifier at the opposite end, so in event Wedding { -- [1] (venue) Place } the property is Wedding::venue. Two things then fix a link, and both are read off what the model already says rather than declared separately. Neither is enforced as an error: the model is not contradictory, it has left {const} unwritten where its own natures settle the matter.

Immutable naturesany end that belongs to one

event, situation, abstract — an event is over, and an abstract individual is outside time. Nothing about either changes afterward, the links they own included. A process is not among them: gaining parts over time is what a process does, so only the ends belonging to the events it has gained are fixed.

Dependencethe target end

@characterizationthe bearer
@mediationthe individuals it mediates
@externalDependencethe individual it depends on
@historicalDependencethe past it depends on

The first three are existential dependence: what depends would have to stop existing to be re-pointed. The fourth is settled differently — what it names has already happened.

Collections that only growa multi-valued end or property

{addOnly} is the weaker of the pair UML 1.x had, beside the frozen that {const} stands for: values may be added and never removed or replaced. With {ordered} it says the stronger thing — what is there now is a prefix of what will be there later — which is axiom A7 of Guarino & Guizzardi’s account of processes. Three things accumulate: what constitutes a process, what makes one up, and the parts of a situation, which changes in no other way.

Essential, inseparable, immutablewhich end, and how rigid

The four meta-properties of Guizzardi’s thesis (§5.4) need no syntax of their own: an end is a property of the classifier at the opposite end, so {const} plus a lower bound at the part end says the whole always has that same part, and at the whole end that the part is always in that whole. Which name applies is read off the rigidity of the type at the other end — essential and inseparable under a rigid one, immutablePart and immutableWhole under an anti-rigid one, where the necessity is only de dicto. A quantity is extensional, so all of its parts are essential and none of it is the modeler’s to choose.

Begin and end pointsthe attribute itself

The same reasoning reaches attributes. @begin and @end name the instants a concrete individual began and ceased to exist — everything but the abstract ones, a high-order type included, since a type is an endurant too. Both are {const}: when something began does not change, and once it has ended, neither does when it ended. @end should be [0..1] on anything that may still be going on — an endurant that exists, a process unfolding, a situation obtaining — while an event, having occurred, has both instants. Both stereotypes on one attribute says the individual is instantaneous. None of it is ever required.

How the rules are built

Forty-odd checks about stereotypes and ontological natures could each be written as its own predicate. That is how this started, and it did not hold: separate rules for the same underlying question drifted apart, and one of them offered a repair that repaired nothing. The rules now rest on a small inference core, and each diagnostic is a reading of the same answer.

One question, asked once

Every restriction on a class is a NatureConstraint, whatever its origin: the stereotype's identity principle, an explicit of clause, or a supertype — whose own natures are derived the same way, so the walk goes all the way up the hierarchy.

constraintsOnCollect what the stereotype, the of clause and every supertype each allow.
possibleNaturesIntersect them. This is what an instance could actually be.
isUnsatisfiable
isWideningEmpty intersection, or an of clause naming what a supertype excludes.
culpritsWhich single constraints, removed, leave a consistent set.

The four nature diagnostics differ only in which constraints they name in the message. The quick fixes for all four share one handler driven by culprits: a culprit of kind supertype becomes a “remove this specialization”, kind declared becomes “remove the nature restriction”, kind stereotype becomes the list of stereotypes the remaining constraints admit.

The general rule is stricter than the hand-written ones it replaced, and correct where they were not. subkind Odd of events specializes Person no longer offers to drop the supertype: doing so leaves subkind Odd of events, which a subkind still cannot be. Two object-natured supertypes under an event are no longer blamed individually, because dropping either leaves the other contradicting the stereotype just as flatly — the whole clause is offered instead.

Completion answers with the same function

Autocomplete never proposes something the validator would immediately flag, because it asks the same question of a hierarchy that does not exist yet. admitsSupertype(subject, candidate) adds the candidate as a constraint and checks the result; admitsStereotype does the same for a stereotype the class does not currently have. A generalization set states the same relation in different syntax, so it is filtered by the same predicate as a specializes clause.

A second core, for classification levels

The multi-level rules rest on their own small graph rather than on syntax. Every @instantiation relation and every instanceOf clause produces one CrossLevel link — a type sitting exactly one order above another — and MLT’s cross-level relations are read off it: isPowertypeLink for Cardelli’s definition, isCharacterizationLink for Odell’s, and partitionsBase for the case the paper reads off the multiplicity at the higher-order end. The twelve rules are then statements about that graph.

Links are gathered workspace-wide, and that turned out to matter more than expected: the first version read only the base type’s own body, so an @instantiation written as a package-level relation — which is how the paper’s own figures write it — was invisible to half these rules.

Tables where the theory is tabular

  • gUFO shapes. Five of the seven SHACL constraints in nemo-ufes/gufoshapes restate rules the generalization table and the nature core already enforce. The other two look at the categorization pattern from the instance side, and are transcribed here.
  • Generalization. Which stereotype may specialize which. This one started as a transcription and stopped being one: all 441 pairs now follow from six principles, and a test asserts that every refusal can name the principle refusing it, so a row edited without a reason behind it fails the build.
  • Relation ends. Which natures each relation stereotype admits at each end, from association_constraints_nature.json. Parthood entries read (whole, part), so the aggregation diamond — not the writing order — decides which class is looked up where.
  • Natures. objects expands to its three atomic members before any comparison, so a supertype restricted to objects and a subtype restricted to functional-complexes compare correctly in both directions.

One traversal, four relations

Inherence, constitution, parthood and instantiation are each irreflexive and asymmetric, and each forbids a cycle for the same reason: following the relation always moves strictly further from where you started, so it cannot lead back. findCycle is written once and every check supplies only the edges it follows.

The bespoke traversals came first; the third was where the pattern became obvious. Moving parthood onto the shared search immediately exposed a direction error the hand-written version had hidden — the successor of a class is what it is a part of, not what it is the whole of — and four existing tests failed on it.

Rules that need the whole workspace

Several rules ask what refers to a type rather than what it declares — a relator must mediate, and mediate two; a moment must have a bearer; a non-sortal must generalize a sortal; a power type must have a base. The relation carrying that dependency is usually written beside the participant, not the type, so a document-local check would report a false positive on every model split across files. IndexManager.findAllReferences supplies a workspace-wide reverse lookup, filtered to what the ontology being checked can see: its own packages and the ontologies it imports, never one that imports it, so a mediation written downstream cannot make a dependency’s role valid. tonto’s index keeps references by their target, so each question is one lookup rather than a pass over every reference in the workspace. Where the workspace is still loading these under-report rather than false-alarm, which is the safe direction for a warning.

Caches that hold resolved references

Ancestor sets, disjointness indexes and derived natures are all computed from resolved cross-references and cached per node. Langium re-links a dependent document without re-parsing it, so a cache keyed on AST nodes will happily serve pre-change answers after an edit in another file. Every such cache registers with registerDerivedCacheReset, wired to DocumentBuilder.onUpdate.

Severity is a claim about certainty

  • Error — the model contradicts itself. No instance could satisfy it, or UML forbids it outright.
  • Warning — defensible but almost certainly unintended, or true only of a model still being written. A relator with no mediations at all is silent; one that mediates exactly one individual is not.
  • Information — the six anti-patterns from Sales & Guizzardi's empirical study. These parse, validate and may be exactly right; they are structures that turned out to correlate with modeling errors. Off unless tonto.antiPatterns asks for them.
  • Hint — the model may say more, and need not. A stereotype the end natures single out, the specialization an override already implies, a whole with one modeled part. Drawn as a faint underline and kept out of the Problems panel, because a document full of plain associations should stay readable.

One vocabulary

There are twenty-three association stereotypes. @creation and @termination state the ends of an endurant’s life, and @change the same relation at a moment in between. Six earlier names were changed, and six earlier tonto extensions were removed. Neither becomes a parse error: any word still follows @, so an older model parses and is answered with the rename — with a quick fix — or with what tonto does instead. The renames have to be stated rather than guessed, since no edit distance connects memberOf to membership.

Dropping the extensions closed a gap as a side effect. The end-nature table covered 18 of 25 stereotypes; six of the seven it missed were those extensions, and the seventh was constitution, whose ends were not stated. All twenty-three are now covered, and a test keeps it that way.

Every example on this page was validated by the language server that ships with this project, and the messages, severities and quick-fix titles are its own output rather than a paraphrase. Rules are identified by the diagnostic code the server attaches, which is also what the code-action provider dispatches on and what --fix= takes by name.
